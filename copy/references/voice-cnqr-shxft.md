# The CNQR / SHXFT house voice

Not a template. Not a tone matrix. A living reference of what our best copy actually sounds like, pulled from live decks that shipped and closed.

## What "our voice" is

**Editorial, not corporate.** We write in complete thoughts, but they're short. Sentences carry weight. There is no filler.

**Confident, not hopeful.** Declarative statements. No hedging ("we think," "arguably," "kind of"). If a claim needs a hedge, we don't make the claim.

**Specific, not aspirational.** Names, numbers, places, moments. "Dubai Islands, on her dining table, before launch" beats "an immersive property experience." "31M plays" beats "massive reach."

**Reader-flattering, not reader-scolding.** We name gaps the reader will nod at. We never make the reader themselves the mistake.

**Rhythm-driven, not uniform.** Short sentences chain. Occasional longer thought breaks the pattern. The reader hears a beat.

**Metaphor when it lands, not when it flourishes.** "Fishing in the dark," "lights the engine," "the lane sits open." Metaphors that do work. Not "our journey to unlock…"

## Gems from live decks (the canonical shortlist)

Structural patterns that made these gems work follow after each cluster. When we write new copy, we borrow structures — not phrases.

### Platform lines (hero H1s)

- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Their building doesn't exist. Their behaviour does."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"The Saudi athlete brand on Snap."*
- **Genie:** *"Snap owns the Gulf's youth."*

**Structure:** short, declarative, one insight. Never a promise, always an observation. The reader supplies the "so what."

### Tension lines (h2s that name the gap)

- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Your sales team is fishing in the dark."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"You didn't enter Saudi. You earned it."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"Instagram is where they look at the world. Snap is where they are Saudi."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"The buyer didn't fill a form yet. The dashboard already knows."*
- **Genie:** *"A Lens isn't a billboard you rent. It's an engine your audience runs for you."* — endorsed by Tia (Snap MENA insider) as the line that lands. Rule: this is the deck's ONE deliberate "not X, it's Y" per the ban limit — other slides in the same deck must avoid the construction.

**Structure:** parallel construction. "X. Y." with the second sentence tilting the first. Names the tension without judging the reader. When there's a "you" it's flattering, never accusing.

**Endorsement rule:** when a line gets a direct positive callout from a real practitioner (Tia at Snap, a client marketing director, a senior planner outside CNQR), that line is elevated to canon regardless of whether it technically matches a /copy structural rule. Rules exist to defend the voice; endorsements ARE the voice. Rewrite around the endorsed line — don't kill it.

### Sub-copy that carries weight (ledes and pull-quotes)

- **SquatWolf:** *"The lane sits open. Global players run media bursts on platform. The premium activewear category barely shows up. The gap isn't a gap. It's a category claim waiting for the brand confident enough to take it."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"Not 'in Saudi.' Not 'for Saudi.' A cultural-ownership claim no global activewear brand can replicate, because none of them have spent the last three years actually earning it. You have. We make that earned position visible, daily, on the platform where it counts."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Marketing analytics tells you page views. Your CRM tells you form-fills. Neither tells you who's about to buy."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Real-time intent scoring across every visitor. Returns weight higher than first-visits. Dwell-time on specific units. AR clip share-outs. Cross-device returns. Time-of-day patterns."*

Second tier — solid, keep as reference for the chained-reveal structure, but not canonical gems:
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Your buyer in London opens her phone. Scans the QR on your Dubai Islands teaser. The tower appears on her dining table, scaled to her room. She walks around it. Taps Floor 22. The unit lights up."* (mechanic explainer, not a hero line)

**Structure:** chained short sentences with a reveal at the end. Or a rhythmic list where each item is concrete. The paragraph has a curve — it doesn't drone.

### Rhythmic three-beats (openers and closers)

- **SquatWolf:** *"Twelve weeks. Two moments. One window."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"Pack Frame. Pick your moment. Capture it."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"One August launch. One September hero. One programme that keeps running when you want it to."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"Two numbers. Both ours. Both real."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Three years. Tier-1 institutions. Shipped from Dubai."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Built by us. Owned by us. The hardware that comes free with your engagement."*
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Capture. Behaviour. Score. Brief. Every minute."*

**Structure:** three or four short beats. Anaphora (repeated openers) or symmetric shapes. Never four beats when three work.

### Reader-flattering closers

- **Peace Homes:** *"Let's make the next launch the one they remember."*
- **Genie:** *"Start small. Scale when it works."*
- **SquatWolf:** *"Lock the August creative by mid-July. The rest follows."*

Second tier — fine but generic ("let's start with X" is a template, not a gem):
- **ARY&MAZ:** *"Let's start with Dubai Islands."*

**Structure:** first-person plural. Concrete. Doesn't beg, doesn't overcommit. The next step is small and named.

### Section labels (kickers) that do work

Genie's chapter markers — clean editorial labels that build tension:
- *"The market truth"*
- *"The blind spot"*
- *"The gap"*
- *"The insight"*
- *"First, the difference"*
- *"Enter Genie"*
- *"The unfair advantage"*
- *"The scale"*
- *"The personal Lens"*

**Structure:** two or three words, no verb, article + noun. Reads as chapter titles. Never "Introducing X" or "Our Approach."

## Voice range (when we push the register)

**Warm-authoritative** — most decks. Used when Hasan wants to be trusted more than he wants to entertain.
> *"We know the platform from both sides of the building. That is the difference between an agency guessing at Snap and the people who built campaigns on it from the inside."*

**Editorial-cool** — SquatWolf, ARY&MAZ. Used when the audience is sophisticated and expects fewer words.
> *"The lane sits open."*

**Punchy-declarative** — three-beats, tension lines. Used when the deck needs to shift energy.
> *"Twelve weeks. Two moments. One window."*

**Cinematic-specific** — story moments in decks. Used when we're showing not telling a mechanic.
> *"Your buyer in London opens her phone. Scans the QR on your Dubai Islands teaser. The tower appears on her dining table, scaled to her room. She walks around it. Taps Floor 22. The unit lights up."*

**Personal-direct** — CTA slides, WhatsApp copy, personal notes.
> *"Let's start with Dubai Islands."*

We do not use: **corporate-formal, marketing-scoldy, aspirational-vague, self-important, over-explanatory.**

## Voice hits and misses

### Hits (what we've written when we're at our best)

- *"Their building doesn't exist. Their behaviour does."* → parallel + tilt + insight in nine words
- *"The lane sits open."* → four words that reframe an entire competitive landscape
- *"Twelve weeks. Two moments. One window."* → three-beat rhythm that carries urgency without saying "urgent"

### Misses (what we've shipped that reads AI)

- *"Now imagine it pointed at your brand."* (Genie) → textbook AI close, call-to-imagination
- *"These are published, not concepts."* (Genie) → defensive throat-clearing a copywriter trusts the reel to do
- *"Because it's fun, people record themselves and post…"* (Genie) → over-explained causation
- *"Amateur brands buy Snap media. Serious brands own Lenses."* (past copy) → judges the reader personally
- *"That is the reach a single Snap Lens can earn on its own."* (Genie) → weak "that is" sentence opener

Every miss is a pattern — not a one-off. The patterns are catalogued in [`ai-tells-hard-bans.md`](ai-tells-hard-bans.md) and [`ai-tells-soft-flags.md`](ai-tells-soft-flags.md).

## Rules that show up nowhere in this doc but the reader can feel

- We don't say *"partner."* We say *"work with."* Partner is a business-development word.
- We don't say *"solutions."* We say what the thing is. Solutions is what people say when they're hiding the noun.
- We don't say *"platform"* casually. Platform is a specific claim (you can build on it). If it's not a platform, say *"tool," "product," "engine."*
- We don't say *"unique."* Everything says unique. We describe the specific thing that makes it unique instead.
- We don't say *"industry-leading" / "best-in-class" / "premium quality."* We name a client, a stat, or a stat + client and let the phrase die.
- We don't say *"passionate about."* No one who is passionate about something says they are.
- We don't say *"end-to-end."* We describe the ends.

---

## The final filter

**Read every line aloud.** If you stumble, rewrite. If you're bored, cut it. If you're proud of the metaphor, kill the sentence around it and see if the metaphor still stands alone. Most of them don't. The ones that do are the gems.
