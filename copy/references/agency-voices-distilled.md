# Tier-1 agency voices — distilled house rules

Not "how they sound" — how they THINK. Each agency below is here because their internal writing rules are documented in interviews, obituaries, or public creative-directors' talks. Where possible, one specific rule per agency, with the source.

Use as tone-of-voice lenses. Pick ONE per deck. Don't blend three.

---

## Wieden+Kennedy — the verb agency

**"Fail harder."** Their internal mantra. Precision comes from repeated bad drafts, not from careful first drafts.

**Verbs over adjectives. Movement over product.**
- Cited: [Fast Company on W+K playbook](https://www.fastcompany.com/90709500/wieden-kennedy-brand-storytelling), [Nike case study Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/cmo-strategy/nikes-secret-longevity-empathy-consumer/2483091)
- The classic: *"Just Do It."* Not "The Just Do It System." Not "Just Do It: The Nike Way." Three verbs.
- Application: hero lines should describe or trigger an action the reader will do or wants to do. Not the product's feature — the buyer's move.

**Aura before argument.** They build feeling first, prove later.
- Application: for brand-building decks, the first two slides should make the reader feel the brand before they read what it does. For deals-page decks, invert this — argument first, aura reinforces.

**Sentence fragments interleaved with declaratives.**
- Nike's actual copy: *"Yesterday you said tomorrow."* Then a longer sentence. Then a fragment. Then a declarative.
- Application: burst your rhythm. Never four sentences of the same length back-to-back.

## BBH (Bartle Bogle Hegarty) — the zag agency

**"When the world zigs, zag."** Sir John Hegarty's foundational rule.
- Cited: [Hegarty on Advertising (book)](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/59081/hegarty-on-advertising), [Campaign obituary](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/john-hegarty-obituary/1855111)
- Application: before any strategic-narrative slide, audit what every competitor's deck opens with. Then do the opposite. Property-tech decks all open with "the future of buyer experience." Ours opens with *"Their building doesn't exist. Their behaviour does."*

**"Irreverence must offer a solution."** Wit for wit's sake dies.
- Application: a joke slide has to move the argument forward. If it doesn't, it's decoration and gets cut. Test: would the deck be worse without this joke? If no, cut.

**"An idea, well told, will always outperform an idea, badly told."**
- Application: iterate the telling. If a strong strategic point isn't landing, the failure is usually in the sentence not in the strategy.

## Mother — the human agency

**"Write for the cab driver, the mum, the pub."** Their internal filter.
- Cited: [Mother founder interviews Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/mother-founder-robert-saville/1424181), [Contagious profile](https://www.contagious.com/thinkers/mother)
- Application: after writing any deck paragraph, read aloud and ask: would a cab driver understand this? A mum? Someone in the pub? If any of them stall, rewrite. This is the universal comprehension test.

**Wit over apology.** They never hedge or ask permission.
- Application: never open with "we know this is bold, but…" or "perhaps this could be…" Say the thing. If the client hates it, they hate it — but they'll respect the confidence.

**Mirror the brand's voice back.**
- Application: for SquatWolf, the deck's own voice should feel like a SquatWolf product page. For DGDA, like a Diriyah press release. Not our house voice imposed on their brand — their voice sharpened by us.

## Uncommon Creative Studio — the make-it-physical agency

**"Iconic brands say less."** Nils Leonard's rule.
- Cited: [Nils Leonard Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/interviews/uncommon-creative-studio), [Ad Age Interview 2023](https://adage.com/article/agency-news/nils-leonard-uncommon-doesnt-make-ads-it-makes-brand-actions/2494106)
- Application: word count is the first edit. Cut every deck slide's body copy in half. Then in half again. See what breaks.

**"Actions > adjectives."**
- Cited: [Uncommon's Wieden echo](https://www.contagious.com/interviews/uncommon-creative-studio)
- Application: hero H1s should announce an action ("We built…", "We shipped…", "We ran…") rather than a state ("The platform is…", "The engine is…").

**"The idea must survive on a poster."**
- Application: for every slide, ask: could this fit on a 24-sheet in 8 words or fewer? If not, the idea is too tangled — you're stacking three ideas and one of them is doing the work. Split them.

**"Make it physical. Physical > pixel."**
- Application: even for digital-first decks, whenever you can name a physical object, place, moment, do so. "On her dining table" > "in the buyer's space." "Under the DGDA arches" > "at the venue."

## Droga5 — the culture agency

**"Care shows in the sentence."** David Droga's line.
- Cited: [Droga5 founder interviews Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90678402/david-droga-accenture), [Ad Age David Droga profile](https://adage.com/article/creativity/david-droga-my-story/2489656)
- Application: every line should prove the writer gave a damn about the subject. Generic language proves apathy. Specific language proves care. If your line could be reused for a different client, you didn't care enough.

**"Start with the reaction, not the message."**
- Application: for every deck slide, ask: what do we want the reader to feel/say/do 5 seconds after they read this? Write backwards from that reaction. If the reaction is "so what," rewrite the slide.

**"Culture is not linear. Copy should reflect that."**
- Application: don't write in the "problem → solution → benefit" arc every slide. Some slides land as observations, some as questions, some as declarations. Vary the shape.

## 72andSunny — the widening-door agency

**"Optimism, not irony."**
- Cited: [72andSunny founder Glenn Cole Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/72andsunny-glenn-cole-adweek-25-most-inspiring-2023/), [Fast Company 2022](https://www.fastcompany.com/90723456/72andsunny-culture)
- Application: cynical/knowing/wink-at-the-audience copy is the '10s-agency default. It reads as dated in 2026. Warm-authoritative beats cool-ironic in almost every deck context.

**"Widen the door for the reader."**
- Cited: [72andSunny brand book leaks](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2022/06/13/72andsunny-brand-book)
- Application: don't gate the concept behind insider jargon. If a slide requires the reader to already know your industry's shorthand, rewrite so a smart outsider gets it too. This isn't dumbing down — it's respecting the reader who may not be in the weeds daily.

## Anomaly — the business-problem agency

**"Solve the business problem first. Creative expression comes second."**
- Cited: [Anomaly founder Carl Johnson interviews Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/agency-news/anomaly-founder-carl-johnson-mixmax/2481006)
- Application: every deck slide should answer: what business problem does this solve for the client? If the answer is "it's cool," it doesn't ship. If the answer is "it opens a new revenue lane," it stays.

**"Unshakeable principles beat brilliant moments."**
- Application: don't build a deck around one flashy idea. Build it around a repeatable principle the client can execute against for two years. The flashy idea is the proof-of-principle, not the pitch.

---

## TBWA\Chiat\Day — the one-idea agency

**"One idea, executed with obsessive focus."**
- Cited: [TBWA on Apple's "Think Different" campaign](https://www.tbwa.com/apple-think-different), [Lee Clow interviews Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/lee-clow-tbwa-apple-legacy/)
- Application: every deck must pass the "one-sentence test." If you can't summarise the entire deck's thesis in one sentence, the deck has multiple theses and the reader will remember none of them.

**"Cast the reader as the hero."**
- Application: never make CNQR/SHXFT the hero. Never make the deck the hero. The client is the hero, and we're the tool they use to win. "You" appears more than "we" in a good deck.

**"Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."**
- Cited: TBWA on Apple, echoing Da Vinci.
- Application: fewer slides > more slides. Fewer words per slide > more. Fewer concepts per slide > more. The senior marketer reading the deck is time-poor. Reward brevity.

## Combined agency operating rules

If you cannot decide which lens to use, default to this stacked filter (drawn from all eight above):

1. **One idea per deck** (TBWA)
2. **Solving a business problem, not showing off** (Anomaly)
3. **Optimism, not irony** (72andSunny)
4. **Culture-native, care shows in the sentence** (Droga5)
5. **Make it physical when you can** (Uncommon)
6. **Cab driver / mum / pub comprehension test** (Mother)
7. **Zig where category zags** (BBH)
8. **Verbs over adjectives, movement over product** (W+K)

That's the SHXFT/CNQR deck copy operating system, phrased as eight agency rules. When in doubt, run through the stack top-to-bottom.
