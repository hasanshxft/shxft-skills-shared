# Load-bearing principles — the rules that show up across all sources

Ranked by cross-cutting weight. Each principle appears in at least three of four source layers: **[C] classic masters**, **[A] tier-1 agencies**, **[D] modern deck-narrative**, **[T] AI-tell literature**.

The higher a rule sits, the more sources cite it and the less negotiable it is.

---

## 1. Specificity beats abstraction. Always. — [C][A][D][T]

Every source in every layer treats this as the founding rule.

- **Classic (Hopkins):** *"Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding like water from a duck. Specific facts have their full weight and effect."*
- **Agency (Uncommon Leonard, W+K):** verbs over adjectives, concrete over aspirational.
- **Deck (Dunford, Laja/Wynter):** stats and named alternatives beat feature language.
- **AI-tell (all sources):** vague-authority ("Industry reports suggest") is a top-3 detector signal.

**Applied:** *"$1.9T processed"* not *"industry-leading."* *"31M plays"* not *"massive reach."* *"Dubai Islands, on her dining table, before launch"* not *"the property, in the buyer's space."*

---

## 2. Cut every third sentence. Then read the result. — [C][A][D][T]

Every layer enforces sentence-cutting as first-pass discipline.

- **Classic (Trott):** *"Impact. Communication. Persuasion — in that order."* 90% of copy dies at Impact because it takes too many words to hit.
- **Agency (Anomaly, TBWA/Clow):** *"Pick the most compelling feature and present it in the most compelling way."* One idea per unit. Kill the second sentence.
- **Deck (First Round, Wynter):** slide headline carries the argument. If the sentence isn't the argument, it's noise.
- **AI-tell:** LLMs default to over-explanation and safety redundancy. Two sentences saying almost the same thing is the fingerprint.

**Applied:** After first draft, read every paragraph and delete every third sentence. If the meaning survives, ship it. Most drafts survive.

---

## 3. Vary sentence length. Rhythm not uniformity. — [C][A][D][T]

The single most reliable AI-detection signal. Also the single most reliable "this reads like a real writer" signal.

- **Classic (Bendinger, Klinkenborg):** rhythm carries the reader through the argument.
- **Agency (W+K on Nike):** sentence fragments interleaved with declaratives.
- **Deck (Superhuman, Linear H1s):** short verb phrases stacked, then broken by longer explanatory line.
- **AI-tell (GPTZero methodology):** "burstiness" score — sentence-length variance — is *the* core detection metric. LLMs default to ~27-word uniform sentences. Humans burst: short-short-long-short.

**Applied:** After first draft, count words per sentence in a paragraph. If most cluster around one length (variance < 50%), break the pattern deliberately. Cut a sentence in half. Combine two into one longer thought. Alternate.

---

## 4. Verbs over adjectives. Actions over decoration. — [C][A][D]

Named explicitly by three of the four layers as the fastest way to sharpen weak copy.

- **Classic (Ogilvy, Bernbach):** the sentence should describe or trigger a doing.
- **Agency (W+K "Just Do It," Uncommon "Actions > adjectives," Linear/Stripe verb-led H1s):** Ship. Own. Compound. Not premium, revolutionary, seamless.
- **Deck:** landing page headlines lead with verbs the buyer does; adjectives (if any) stack on outcome verbs.

**Applied:** Scan any paragraph for adjective density. Replace 60%+ of adjectives with the verb they were decorating. *"Robust analytics"* → *"Analytics that hold up."* *"Seamless integration"* → *"Plugs in. No engineering ticket."*

---

## 5. Never position the reader as ignorant. — [A][D][T]

Not in the classic-masters layer directly (they wrote to consumers, not B2B buyers). Universally load-bearing in agency + deck + AI-tell layers.

- **Agency (Droga5, Mother):** cast the reader as the hero. Sophisticated audience. Don't explain their own business back to them.
- **Deck (Raskin):** the enemy is the status quo (the old worldview, the old process). Never the reader themselves. Never a competitor by name.
- **AI-tell:** LLMs default to explaining basics because they can't tell if the reader is a novice or an expert. Every AI-written B2B copy over-explains.

**Applied:** Name category-level truths the reader will nod at. Never say *"most brands are amateur"* (implicates reader). Say *"most Snap spend goes to media. The compounding asset is a Lens library"* (category observation, reader supplies the "so what").

---

## 6. Cut the throat-clearing openers. — [C][A][T]

Every source flags formulaic openers as amateur.

- **Classic (Sugarman):** first sentence's only job is to earn the second. Introductory throat-clearing kills the slippery slide.
- **Agency (Uncommon, Mother):** wit over apology. If the sensible move is a formal opener, write the human sentence instead.
- **AI-tell:** *"In today's landscape," "In a world where," "Now imagine," "Simply put," "Let's dive into," "Whether you're X or Y"* — top-10 detector openers.

**Applied:** If a paragraph opens with an adverbial phrase, cut it. Start with the noun. Start with the verb. Start with the number.

---

## 7. Trust the reader to close the loop. — [C][A][D]

Modernity separates copy from over-narration.

- **Classic (Bernbach):** *"Get out of the way of your product."*
- **Agency (Droga5, Uncommon):** copy is cultural, not linear. Lines that get screenshotted travel further than lines that explain themselves.
- **Deck:** senior marketers reading a pitch deck hate being narrated to. "The takeaway is…" reads as vendor pandering.

**Applied:** Delete *"Now imagine…"*, *"The takeaway is…"*, *"In other words…"*, *"What this means for you is…"*. If the point needs restating, the first statement was wrong — rewrite the first statement instead.

---

## 8. When the category zigs, zag. — [A][D]

BBH's foundational rule, echoed by every deck-narrative source.

- **Agency (Hegarty/BBH):** *"When the world zigs, zag."* If everyone in the category is loud, whisper. If everyone is warm, cut cold.
- **Deck (Raskin, Dunford):** name a shift the category is missing. Don't join the chorus.

**Applied:** Before writing an opener, ask: what does every deck in this category open with? Do the opposite. Property tech decks all open with "the future of buyer experience." Ours opens with *"Their building doesn't exist. Their behaviour does."*

---

## 9. First sentence earns the second. — [C][T]

Sugarman's slippery slide, reinforced by every AI-tell detector.

- **Classic (Sugarman):** *"The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get you to read the second sentence."*
- **AI-tell:** LLMs default to context-establishing first sentences ("Marketing is evolving rapidly…"). Human writers open with something that makes the reader lean in — a stat, a punch, a specific.

**Applied:** Draft the first sentence last. Or draft ten first sentences and pick the one you'd read past.

---

## 10. Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite. — [C][A]

The universal final filter.

- **Classic (Ogilvy):** *"Write the way you talk. Naturally."*
- **Agency (Mother):** *"Write for the cab driver, the mum, the pub."*

**Applied:** Every paragraph, before it ships, read aloud. If you stumble — even once — the copy has a seam. Rewrite until it flows spoken. This is the single most reliable filter because bad copy sounds bad, always, when spoken.

---

## The unranked backup rules (each appears in one to two layers, worth knowing)

- **A joke that doesn't move the argument is dead weight** (Hegarty). Irreverence is a technique, not a decoration.
- **Copy should transmit emotion in as few words as possible** (Wieden). If it needs a paragraph, it's not landing.
- **Care shows in the sentence** (Droga). Every line proves the writer gave a damn about the subject.
- **The idea must survive on a poster** (Uncommon Leonard). If it can't stand alone as a physical object, it's a caption not copy.
- **Words work in the head, pictures work in the gut** (Bendinger). Copy that fights the visual loses.
- **Contrast drives narrative** (Duarte). Every slide oscillates between "what is" and "what could be."
- **Name the shift, not the brand** (Raskin). Decks lead with the world tilting, not the vendor introducing itself.
- **Density scales with reader control** (Dunford). Poster: 7 words. Landing: 15 + subhead. Deck slide: layered.

---

## The application heuristic

When editing existing copy, run the ten load-bearing rules in this order:

1. Cut every third sentence (rule 2)
2. Vary the sentence lengths that remain (rule 3)
3. Replace adjectives with verbs (rule 4)
4. Remove throat-clearing openers (rule 6)
5. Remove "trust the reader" violations (rule 7)
6. Check every sentence for specificity (rule 1)
7. Check for reader-scolding (rule 5)
8. Check that the opener earns the follow (rule 9)
9. Check that the opener isn't category-default (rule 8)
10. Read aloud (rule 10)

Ninety percent of copy fixes happen in steps 1-4. The other six checks catch the remaining edge cases.
